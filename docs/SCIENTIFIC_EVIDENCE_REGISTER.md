# NeuroForge Scientific Evidence Register

**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-06  
**Purpose:** Record the research basis and limitations behind NeuroForge product decisions. This is a working evidence register, not a systematic review or a claim that NeuroForge itself has been validated.

## How to read this register

- **Framework evidence** supports how an ability can be defined or organized. It does not show that a NeuroForge exercise improves it.
- **Training evidence** concerns what interventions changed in a study. Pay attention to population, outcome similarity, study quality and follow-up.
- **Transfer** means improvement on something beyond the trained exercise. Near transfer is similar to the trained task; far transfer is more distinct or practical.
- Evidence ratings below are qualitative product guidance, not formal GRADE ratings.

## Core sources

### 1. Cattell–Horn–Carroll theory of cognitive abilities

- **Source:** Flanagan, D. P. (2014). *The Cattell–Horn–Carroll Theory of Cognitive Abilities*. Wiley Encyclopedia of Special Education.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118660584.ese0431
- **Type:** Scholarly reference chapter / cognitive-ability framework.
- **Product relevance:** A broad, hierarchical framework for organizing cognitive abilities, including fluid reasoning, comprehension-knowledge, memory, visual and auditory processing, processing speed, reaction/decision speed, reading/writing and quantitative knowledge.
- **Limitations:** CHC is a taxonomy/theory of ability structure, not a training recipe. Lists and labels vary across versions. A game collection mapped to CHC is not a CHC assessment.
- **Design implication:** Use CHC-informed domains as NeuroForge's cognitive foundation, and clearly identify any broader applied skills as additional tracks.

### 2. Stability and structure of cognitive abilities

- **Source:** *The Stability of Cognitive Abilities: A Meta-Analytic Review of Longitudinal Studies* (2024; full text indexed in PMC).
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11626988/
- **Type:** Meta-analytic review of longitudinal research.
- **Product relevance:** Summarizes definitions of broad CHC abilities and illustrates that ability constructs have distinct meanings and developmental patterns.
- **Limitations:** Stability across time is not the same question as whether an app can train a construct. Findings do not validate NeuroForge tasks or scoring.
- **Design implication:** Keep cognitive abilities distinct in the profile and avoid collapsing everything into one "brain score."

### 3. Working-memory training transfer

- **Source:** *The asymmetry of working memory training transfer: A systematic review and meta-analysis* (2026).
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13553679/
- **Type:** Three-level meta-analysis of 55 studies, 208 effect sizes and 3,492 participants.
- **Finding:** Updating training transferred to span tasks with a small effect (g = 0.176); span training did not reliably transfer to updating tasks (g = 0.048, not statistically significant).
- **Limitations:** Results concern particular working-memory task relationships, not general intelligence or everyday functioning.
- **Design implication:** Training task design and cognitive demand matter. Do not generalize from one memory exercise to a broad memory or intelligence claim.

### 4. Children's executive-function training transfer

- **Source:** Kassai, R., Futo, J., Demetrovics, Z., & Takacs, Z. K. (2019). *A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence on the near- and far-transfer effects among children's executive function skills*. Psychological Bulletin, 145(2), 165–188.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652908/
- **Type:** Meta-analysis.
- **Finding:** Reported a significant near-transfer effect (g+ = 0.44) and no convincing far-transfer evidence (g+ = 0.11, p = .11) across the included studies.
- **Limitations:** Focused on children's executive-function training and studies available at the time; it does not settle every newer intervention or age group.
- **Design implication:** Test near and far transfer separately; avoid assuming that improving one EF task improves all executive functions.

### 5. Computerized cognitive training in typically developing children and adolescents

- **Source:** Luis-Ruiz, S., et al. (2020). *Is cognitive training an effective tool for improving cognitive function and real-life behaviour in healthy children and adolescents? A systematic review*. Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 116, 268–282.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32565174/
- **Type:** Systematic review of 16 randomized controlled studies.
- **Finding:** Reported near-transfer findings for working memory, while far-transfer findings to other cognitive functions and real-life measures were inconsistent.
- **Limitations:** The evidence base was relatively small and heterogeneous, and long-term transfer was uncertain.
- **Design implication:** Build practical transfer checks and delayed follow-ups into the product rather than relying on game scores.

### 6. Retrieval, spacing and interleaving in classrooms

- **Source:** *A Meta-Analysis of the Effectiveness and Boundary Conditions of Retrieval Practice, Distributed Practice, and Interleaved Practice in Authentic School Classrooms* (2026), Educational Psychology Review.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5
- **Type:** Three-level meta-analysis of 53 studies, 7,303 participants and 233 effect sizes from classroom research published 2006–2024.
- **Finding:** Average effects were g = 0.37 for retrieval practice, g = 0.20 for spacing and g = 0.40 for interleaving; moderators affected the results.
- **Limitations:** Effects depended on learning outcome, material, timing, format and feedback. The authors note limits from sparse moderator cells and available classroom studies.
- **Design implication:** Incorporate recall, spaced review and thoughtful interleaving, but personalize them to the content and learner rather than applying them as rigid universal rules.

### 7. Exercise and cognition

- **Source:** *Effectiveness of exercise for improving cognition, memory and executive function: a systematic umbrella review and meta-meta-analysis*. British Journal of Sports Medicine (2025).
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108589
- **Type:** Umbrella review of 133 systematic reviews, covering 2,724 randomized trials and 258,279 participants.
- **Finding:** The review reported statistically significant pooled benefits for general cognition (SMD = 0.42), memory (SMD = 0.26) and executive function (SMD = 0.24).
- **Limitations:** The pooled literature spans different populations, exercise types and outcomes. It does not mean a specific NeuroForge exercise or workout has the same effect.
- **Design implication:** Include supportive, optional physical-activity education as a cognitive-health context—not as an app intelligence score or medical prescription.

### 8. Adolescent sleep guidance

- **Source:** U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), *Sleep* (updated 2026-09-18).
- **Link:** https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/youth-health-in-focus/sleep.html
- **Type:** Public-health guidance summarizing adolescent sleep recommendations.
- **Finding:** Adolescents ages 13–18 are recommended to get 8–10 hours of sleep per night; sufficient sleep supports attention, learning readiness and health.
- **Limitations:** Public-health guidance is not an intervention trial of NeuroForge.
- **Design implication:** Do not create streak mechanics that encourage late-night overuse. Offer neutral sleep education and encourage healthy routines.

### 9. Social-cognition training

- **Source:** *A systematic review and meta-analysis of social cognition training success across the healthy lifespan* (2022).
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35241715/
- **Type:** Systematic review of 23 intervention studies (N = 1,835); 12 randomized trials (N = 875) contributed to meta-analysis.
- **Finding:** The strongest examined signal concerned theory-of-mind training, while authors called for more research on transfer to other social-cognitive processes and domains.
- **Limitations:** Small numbers of trials in some age groups and outcomes; transfer remains an open question.
- **Design implication:** Build context-rich perspective-taking practice, but do not claim it makes users universally better at reading people.

### 10. Creativity training

- **Source:** *The impact of creativity training on creative performance: A meta-analytic review and critical evaluation of 5 decades of creativity training studies* (2024), Psychological Bulletin, 150(5), 554–585.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000432
- **Type:** Meta-analysis of 169 studies and 844 effect sizes.
- **Finding:** The unadjusted estimated effect was 0.53 SD; adjustment methods accounting for publication bias lowered estimates to approximately 0.29–0.32 SD.
- **Limitations:** The authors identified strong publication-bias concerns and methodological shortcomings that limit confidence in the published literature.
- **Design implication:** Creative-thinking tasks are reasonable to explore, but assess novelty and usefulness with transparent rubrics and conservative evidence labels.

### 11. Executive-function assessment

- **Source:** *Executive functions in research and practice: a multimethod review of behavioral, subjective, and neurobiological assessment tools* (2026), Frontiers in Psychology.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992236/
- **Type:** Multimethod review.
- **Product relevance:** Describes inhibition, working-memory updating and cognitive flexibility as core executive functions; notes that assessment is multidimensional and context-dependent.
- **Limitations:** A narrative review is not a validation study of NeuroForge.
- **Design implication:** Use multiple task formats and avoid treating one short computer game as a complete executive-function assessment.

### 12. Multiple-intelligences theory and measurement caution

- **Source:** *Why multiple intelligences theory is a neuromyth* (2023), Frontiers in Psychology.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1217288
- **Type:** Critical conceptual review.
- **Product relevance:** Reviews critiques concerning the independence, standard measurement and proposed neural separation of Gardner's multiple-intelligences categories.
- **Limitations:** This is a critical perspective in an active scholarly debate; it should not be used to dismiss the importance of diverse talents or domains of achievement.
- **Design implication:** NeuroForge can support a wide variety of human capabilities without claiming that every popular category is a separate, scientifically established intelligence.

### 13. Emotional-intelligence measurement

- **Source:** *The Measurement of Emotional Intelligence: A Critical Review of the Literature and Recommendations for Researchers and Practitioners* (2019).
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31191383/
- **Type:** Critical measurement review.
- **Product relevance:** Distinguishes ability-based, trait and mixed-model approaches and reviews the complexity of available instruments.
- **Limitations:** Different models and tests do not measure one interchangeable construct.
- **Design implication:** Label emotional-skill features precisely; do not merge self-report traits, emotion recognition and ability tests into one unqualified EI score.

## Evidence gaps to keep visible

- A brain-training app's broad effect on general intelligence or "maximum capacity" is not established by these sources.
- Far transfer and durable real-world transfer are much less certain than improvement on trained or similar tasks.
- The same task can measure multiple processes and can be affected by language, education, sensory access, motor response, device characteristics and familiarity.
- NeuroForge needs its own reliability, validity, accessibility and intervention studies before making efficacy claims.

## Maintenance policy

Review this register before adding new training claims, at least once per major release, and whenever a source is superseded or materially challenged. New evidence should be logged with population, method, outcomes, limitations and the exact product decision it changes. Do not upgrade a feature's evidence label based solely on a single small study or vendor marketing.
